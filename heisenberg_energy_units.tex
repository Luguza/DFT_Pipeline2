\noindent
\textbf{Scope.} This note traces what happens to the energy --- and its units ---
from the moment we hand structures and \textbf{total energies} to pymatgen's
\code{HeisenbergMapper}, through construction of the Heisenberg design matrix, the
linear fit itself, and finally into the \code{HeisenbergModel} object that gets
written out. The upstream glue (pulling structures/energies out of the
magnetic-orderings document and reconstructing total energies) is deliberately
omitted: we start where the class's public contract starts, i.e.\ assuming we
already have

\begin{itemize}
  \item a list of magnetic structures (each with a \code{magmom} site property), and
  \item a list of \textbf{total energies} $E_k^{\mathrm{tot}}$ (eV) --- the energy of
        the entire supercell of ordering $k$, summed over all of its atoms.
\end{itemize}

\noindent
All file/line references are to
\code{.venv/\allowbreak lib/\allowbreak python3.12/\allowbreak site-packages/\allowbreak pymatgen/\allowbreak analysis/\allowbreak magnetism/\allowbreak heisenberg.py}.

\noindent
The Heisenberg energy model the code assumes (collinear; the moments enter as
dimensionless scalars, i.e.\ their bare values in $\mu_B$, so $J$ carries the energy
units):
\begin{equation}
E^{\mathrm{tot}} = E_0 - \sum_{\langle i,j\rangle} J_{ij}\, s_i\, s_j ,
\end{equation}
where the sum runs over unique neighbour bonds $\langle i,j\rangle$.

\paragraph{Important convention.}
Nowhere in this pipeline is energy normalised by the \emph{total atom count}. The
only normalisation that ever happens is division by the number of \textbf{magnetic
ions}, $N_k^{\mathrm{mag}}$. So the natural energy unit inside the mapper is
\textbf{eV / mag ion}, and the fitted couplings come out \textbf{per mag ion} ---
\emph{not} per (total) atom --- despite what the source docstrings call
``meV/atom''.

\section*{Step 1 --- Ingestion and normalisation: total energy $\to$ eV / mag ion}

\code{HeisenbergMapper.\_\_init\_\_} immediately routes the inputs through
\code{HeisenbergScreener}, whose \code{\_do\_cleanup} does the unit-relevant work
(\code{heisenberg.py:696}).

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Strip to the magnetic sublattice} (\code{heisenberg.py:717--722}).
        Each structure is reduced to magnetic ions only via
        \code{get\_structure\_with\_only\_magnetic\_atoms}. After this,
        \code{len(s)} for a structure equals $N_k^{\mathrm{mag}}$ --- the
        magnetic-ion count, not the total-atom count.

  \item \textbf{Normalise the energy} (\code{heisenberg.py:725}):
\begin{lstlisting}
energies = [e / len(s) for (e, s) in zip(energies, ordered_structures)]
\end{lstlisting}
        This is the single, decisive unit conversion:
        \begin{equation}
          \varepsilon_k = \frac{E_k^{\mathrm{tot}}}{N_k^{\mathrm{mag}}}
          \qquad [\text{eV}] \;\longrightarrow\; [\text{eV} / \text{mag ion}] .
        \end{equation}
        From here on, every energy the mapper touches is in \textbf{eV / mag ion}.

  \item \textbf{De-duplicate and sort} (\code{heisenberg.py:732--754}). Degenerate
        orderings are dropped using a tolerance of $10^{-6}$ eV / mag ion, then
        structures and energies are sorted ascending in $\varepsilon_k$.
\end{enumerate}

The cleaned, per-mag-ion energies are stored on the mapper as \code{self.energies}
(\code{heisenberg.py:83}). The original total energies are not retained.

\section*{Step 2 --- Building the Heisenberg design matrix (\code{\_get\_exchange\_df})}

\code{\_get\_exchange\_df} (\code{heisenberg.py:229}) builds one linear equation per
ordering. Each row has an \code{E} column (the left-hand side), an \code{E0} column
(coefficient of the reference energy), and one column per neighbour class $ab$ (the
coefficient of $J_{ab}$).

\paragraph{The \code{E} column (LHS).} \code{heisenberg.py:330}:
\begin{lstlisting}
ex_row.loc[sgraph_index, "E"] = self.energies[e_index]
\end{lstlisting}
so the LHS of row $k$ is $\varepsilon_k$, in \textbf{eV / mag ion}.

\paragraph{The coupling columns (design coefficients).} For ordering $k$, the code
loops over every magnetic ion $i$ and every neighbour $j$, accumulating
$-s_i s_j$ into the column for that bond class (\code{heisenberg.py:318--321}).
Because the node loop visits each physical bond twice (once from each endpoint), the
raw column equals $-2\sum s_i s_j$; the explicit $\tfrac12$ factor at
\code{heisenberg.py:338} (\code{ex\_mat[j\_columns] /= 2}) removes the double-count.
The resulting coefficient is
\begin{equation}
  C_{ab,k} = -\!\!\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle \,\in\, ab \text{ in cell } k}\!\! s_i\, s_j
  \qquad (\text{a pure number}) .
\end{equation}
This coefficient is \textbf{extensive}: it is a sum over all bonds of class $ab$ in
the supercell, so it scales with $N_k^{\mathrm{mag}}$.

\paragraph{The \code{E0} column.} Set to $1$ for every row
(\code{heisenberg.py:339}) --- a dimensionless constant carrying the
spin-independent reference energy.

So row $k$ encodes the equation
\begin{equation}
  \varepsilon_k = E_0\cdot 1 + \sum_{ab} J_{ab}\, C_{ab,k} ,
\end{equation}
$J_{ab}$ carries no unit of its own a priori; its dimension is \emph{fixed} by this
balance. Because $C_{ab,k}$ is a pure number, the $J_{ab}$ term must already be in
eV / mag ion:
\begin{equation}
  \underbrace{\Big[\tfrac{\text{eV}}{\text{mag ion}}\Big]}_{\varepsilon_k}
  = \underbrace{\Big[\tfrac{\text{eV}}{\text{mag ion}}\Big]}_{E_0}
  + [J_{ab}]\cdot\underbrace{(\text{number})}_{C_{ab,k}}
  \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\;
  [J_{ab}] = \frac{\text{eV}}{\text{mag ion}} .
\end{equation}

Finally the matrix is made square (\code{heisenberg.py:342--349}): all-zero columns
are dropped\footnote{\textbf{The singularity guard (\code{heisenberg.py:341--345})
is broken, in a pandas-version-dependent way.} It does anything only when a coupling
column is all-zero --- a degenerate case where some interaction class has
$\sum s_i s_j = 0$ in \emph{every} retained ordering --- so ordinary multi-$J$ fits
never enter it. When it does fire there are two faults. (i)~It removes only the
\emph{first} all-zero column, because \code{c = ex\_mat.columns[zeros.index(True)]}
uses \code{list.index(True)} (first match); so under the pandas the code was written
for ($\le 2$.x), two or more all-zero columns leave $H$ singular at
\code{np.linalg.inv(H)} (\code{heisenberg.py:377}). (ii)~On the \textbf{pandas 3.0.3}
pinned in this project the call \code{ex\_mat.drop(columns=[c], axis=1)} passes both
\code{columns} and \code{axis}, which now raises
\code{ValueError: Cannot specify both 'axis' and 'index'/'columns'} (verified) --- so
it deletes \emph{nothing} and the fit aborts on the very first all-zero column. The
commented-out \code{ex\_mat.drop(ex\_mat.tail(len\_zeros).index)} on the next line
hints that removing all of them was the intent.} and the rows are truncated so the
number of equations equals the number of unknowns ($E_0$ plus the retained $J_{ab}$).

\section*{Step 3 --- The fit itself (\code{get\_exchange})}

\code{get\_exchange} (\code{heisenberg.py:353}) solves the square linear system
assembled above. Writing the unknown vector
$x = (E_0, J_{\mathrm{nn}}, J_{\mathrm{nnn}}, \dots)^{\mathsf T}$, the design matrix
$H$ (the \code{E0} + coupling columns), and the right-hand side
$\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} = (\varepsilon_1, \dots, \varepsilon_n)^{\mathsf T}$:
\begin{equation}
  H\, x = \boldsymbol{\varepsilon} .
\end{equation}
Solved by inversion (\code{heisenberg.py:376--378}):
\begin{lstlisting}
H_inv = np.linalg.inv(H)
j_ij  = H_inv @ E      # E here is the eps column, in eV / mag ion
\end{lstlisting}

\paragraph{Units coming out of the solve.} $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}$ is in
eV / mag ion and the coupling columns $C_{ab,k}$ are pure numbers, so the raw solution
components have units
\begin{equation}
  [E_0] = \frac{\text{eV}}{\text{mag ion}},
  \qquad
  [J_{ab}] = \frac{\text{eV}}{\text{mag ion}} ,
\end{equation}
i.e.\ each $J_{ab}$ is an exchange energy expressed \textbf{per magnetic ion}
(because the LHS was per mag ion while the design coefficients were left extensive
--- see the consistency note in Step~6).

\paragraph{Conversion to meV.} \code{heisenberg.py:381}:
\begin{lstlisting}
j_ij[1:] *= 1000      # eV -> meV ; the [0] entry (E_0) is left in eV
\end{lstlisting}
so the reported couplings are
\begin{equation}
  J_{ab} \;\text{in}\; \frac{\text{meV}}{\text{mag ion}}
  \qquad (\text{source labels this ``meV/atom''}).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Degenerate case (fewer than 3 couplings).} If the symmetry analysis
yields fewer than three independent neighbour classes, the matrix route is skipped
(\code{heisenberg.py:367--373}) and a single averaged $\langle J\rangle$ from
Step~3a is returned instead.

\subsection*{Step 3a --- The $\langle J\rangle$ shortcut (\code{estimate\_exchange})}

\code{estimate\_exchange} (\code{heisenberg.py:454}) bypasses the matrix and
estimates one average coupling from the lowest FM and AFM orderings, using the same
per-mag-ion energies. With $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{fm}}$, $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{afm}}$
taken from \code{self.energies} (\code{heisenberg.py:468}):
\begin{align}
  \Delta\varepsilon &= \varepsilon_{\mathrm{afm}} - \varepsilon_{\mathrm{fm}}
    & & [\text{eV} / \text{mag ion}] \\
  m_{\mathrm{avg}} &= \big\langle |s_i| \big\rangle_{\mathrm{FM}}
    & & (\text{a pure number, in } \mu_B) \\
  \langle J\rangle &= \Delta\varepsilon \,/\, m_{\mathrm{avg}}^2
    & & [\text{eV} / \text{mag ion}] \\
  \langle J\rangle &\mathrel{*}= 1000
    & & [\text{meV} / \text{mag ion}]
    \quad (\code{heisenberg.py:485--487})
\end{align}
So $\langle J\rangle$ (stored later as \code{javg}) is, like the matrix couplings,
an exchange energy \textbf{per magnetic ion}, in meV.

\section*{Step 5 --- Units summary (end to end)}

\begin{lstlisting}[style=plain]
INPUT                 Step 1                   Steps 2-3            OUTPUT
E_k^tot               eps_k = E_k^tot/N_k^mag  fit  H x = eps      ex_params, javg
[eV, whole cell] ---> [eV / mag ion]      ---> J_ab x1000    --->  [meV / mag ion]
                      (heisenberg.py:725)      (heisenberg.py:381) model.energies = eps_k
                                                                   [eV / mag ion]
\end{lstlisting}

The whole mapper lives in \textbf{two} energy units only: \textbf{eV / mag ion} (the
cleaned energies and the matrix LHS) and \textbf{meV / mag ion} (the reported
couplings). Total atom counts never enter; the only normalisation is by
$N_k^{\mathrm{mag}}$.

\section*{Step 6 --- Consistency caveat: the supercells must match in size}

The fit divides the \textbf{left-hand side} by $N_k^{\mathrm{mag}}$ (Step~1) but
leaves the \textbf{design coefficients} $C_{ab,k}$ extensive (summed over all bonds,
Step~2). Putting those two facts together, the system the code actually solves is
\begin{equation}
  \frac{E_k^{\mathrm{tot}}}{N_k^{\mathrm{mag}}}
  = E_0 + \sum_{ab} J_{ab}\, C_{ab,k} .
\end{equation}
Multiplying back by $N_k^{\mathrm{mag}}$ and comparing to the physical total-energy
model
\begin{equation}
  E_k^{\mathrm{tot}} = E_0^{\mathrm{tot}} + \sum_{ab} J_{ab}^{\mathrm{phys}}\, C_{ab,k}
\end{equation}
shows the two agree, for \textbf{all} rows simultaneously, only if
$N_k^{\mathrm{mag}}$ is the \textbf{same for every ordering}. In that case the solved
couplings equal the physical ones divided by that common magnetic-ion count --- which
is exactly why they are ``per mag ion.''

\paragraph{It is the \emph{absolute} supercell size that matters, not the primitive
cell.} Write the supercell of ordering $k$ as $n_k$ primitive cells, with $\nu$
magnetic ions per primitive cell ($\nu$ is a constant of the material). Then
$N_k^{\mathrm{mag}} = \nu\, n_k$, and because $C_{ab,k}$ is a raw sum over bonds it
scales with the supercell, $C_{ab,k} = n_k\, c_{ab,k}$, where $c_{ab,k}$ is the
per-primitive-cell coefficient. The fitted row then reads
\begin{equation}
  \underbrace{\varepsilon_k}_{\text{intensive }(\propto\, n_k^0)}
  = E_0 + \sum_{ab} J_{ab}\,\underbrace{n_k\, c_{ab,k}}_{C_{ab,k}\,\propto\, n_k} .
\end{equation}
The left side is intensive (energy per magnetic ion, independent of $n_k$), yet every
exchange term on the right carries an explicit $n_k$. A single
$(E_0, \{J_{ab}\})$ can satisfy all rows only if $n_k$ --- the absolute supercell
size, hence $N_k^{\mathrm{mag}}$ --- is the same throughout. The per-primitive-cell
count $\nu$ is constant by construction and does \emph{not} relax this.

\paragraph{The reference term is the exception.} The nonmagnetic energy is genuinely
extensive, $E_{0,k}^{\mathrm{tot}} = n_k\, e_0$, so
$E_{0,k}^{\mathrm{tot}}/N_k^{\mathrm{mag}} = e_0/\nu$ is intensive and fits a single
$E_0$ for \emph{any} $n_k$ (its column is simply $1$). So the ``$E^{\mathrm{tot}}$ is
an integer multiple of the primitive-cell energy'' intuition rescues the reference
term --- but not the exchange terms: for a non-FM ordering the magnetic period is the
larger magnetic cell, the exchange contribution differs per ordering, and that
ordering-dependent piece is exactly the size-sensitive signal being fit.

\paragraph{What would remove the restriction.} If the code also divided the design
coefficients by $N_k^{\mathrm{mag}}$ (using $c_{ab,k}/\nu$, a per-mag-ion
coefficient), the whole row would be intensive and the solve would return
$J_{ab}^{\mathrm{solved}} = J_{ab}^{\mathrm{phys}}$ for \emph{any} mix of supercell
sizes. The limitation is precisely the asymmetry: the energy (LHS) is normalised to
per mag ion, while the design matrix (RHS) is left extensive.

\paragraph{In practice.} For orderings enumerated from a single parent cell --- the
usual magnetic-orderings workflow --- all supercells share one size, the condition
holds, and the couplings come out uniformly as $J^{\mathrm{phys}}/N^{\mathrm{mag}}$
(the ``per mag ion'' of the reported units). It is only a hazard if supercells of
different sizes are mixed into a single fit.
